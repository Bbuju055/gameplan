# Narrative Pulse: the two-minute guide

**What it is.** A regular check on what AI engines say when someone asks about our client or the
class it competes in. Claude is asked every time. ChatGPT and Perplexity are asked too where they can
be reached automatically; in the Claude app they can't, so the check is named for what it measured:
"What Claude says about" the client. Scored against the client folder, so
"accurate" means accurate to what the client has approved, not to what sounds right.

**Why it matters.** Investors, partners' business-development teams, and journalists now start with
an engine. If the engine leaves the client out of the class, tells an old story, or repeats a claim
the client never approved, that is the first impression we are working against. Pulse tells us
where we stand before a readout.

## How it runs

1. **The first check runs when the client is set up** (or the first time you type `/pulse` and the
   client's name). It writes usually 12 to 20 plain questions a buyer would type (fewer when no confirmed date is ahead), drawn from the client
   folder and the landscape, and asks them straight away. Nobody has to read and confirm them:
   they're all listed in the page, and you change them any time by saying so (*add a question: is
   Acme a credible partner?*, *drop the one about kidney cancer*). The first check is the
   baseline: the first measurement, kept so later checks can be compared with it. That
   comparison isn't made yet; each check is read on its own.
2. **After that, Gameplan suggests the next check** when you ask what's due: a week before each
   big date, and about monthly otherwise.

## What you will see at the review

- **Five dimensions**, one line each: is the client named at all (presence), is what the engines say
  true to the folder (accuracy), which story are they telling (framing), how much of the class
  conversation the client gets (share of voice), and who the engines cite (citations).
- **Findings**, each with the engine's exact words next to the line in the client folder it was
  checked against. You accept, dispute, or add a note, then approve once; your name from setup goes
  on it.
- A claim the client has not approved stays flagged even if an engine repeats it. It's fixed by
  approving the claim for the client, not in the review.

## What you get

The Pulse report: one page with the five dimensions, the three findings that matter, what to fix
at the source (a page on the client's own site to update, another outlet to ask for a correction,
or news the engines will likely catch up with), and the next run date, plus an appendix with every
question and the engines' words behind each finding. It's saved in `Deliverables`, then the client's name, and your
approval of the findings is its approval: nothing asks again. Two lists travel with it: outlets the
engines cite, which the next target list for this client favours, and companies the engines name
that we are not tracking, to look at when the landscape is next refreshed. What the engines get wrong can also become the
angle of the next pitch.

## Plain rules

- Pulse measures. It never writes for the engines, seeds content, or optimises pages.
- "In the same window as" our coverage, never "because of" it.
- Claude is asked on the firm's own account, so account settings may shape its answers; the report
  says so. It shows what Claude says here, not exactly what a member of the public sees.
- Gemini and Google AI Overviews are not measured, by decision; the report says so.
